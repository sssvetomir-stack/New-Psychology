From knowledge to action

Knowledge in itself does not change a person.

A person develops only when knowledge becomes thoughts, thoughts become decisions, decisions become actions, and actions become habits.

Practice is the main tool of the New Psychology DREVO.

Its goal is not to change a person overnight, but to create a way of life in which development becomes a natural process.

The first principle

Every day a person can do at least one action that will strengthen life.

This action may be aimed at:

  • body;→
  • psyche;→
  • consciousness;→
  • will;→
  • soul;→
  • spirit;→
  • relationship;→
  • nature;→
  • society.→

Small daily actions gradually change the entire human system.

The second principle

  • Develop all levels simultaneously.
  • Like a tree, it is impossible to develop just one part.
  • The roots feed the trunk.
  • The trunk supports the branches.
  • The branches produce leaves.
  • Leaves feed the tree.
  • The fruits become the seeds of new life.
  • A person develops in the same way.

If only the body is strengthened but relationships are destroyed, development becomes one-sided.

If intelligence develops but values ​​are lost, knowledge can be used for harm.

Harmonious development requires attention to all levels of personality.

The third principle

  • Practice is more important than intention.
  • Intention shows direction.
  • Practice creates result.

It is better to do one useful action today than to make great plans that will never be realized.

The fourth principle

  • Development begins with oneself.
  • It is impossible to teach health by destroying your own body.
  • It is impossible to cultivate honesty by constantly lying.
  • It is impossible to talk about love without showing respect for people.
  • A man becomes an example before he becomes a teacher.

The fifth principle

Leave the world a little better every day.

This could be:

  • helping a person;
  • new knowledge;
  • planted tree;
  • restored health;
  • created work;
  • problem solved;
  • a kind word;
  • an honest act.

Creation comes from small daily actions.

The sixth principle

  • Regularity is more important than intensity.
  • A one-time effort rarely changes a life.
  • Constant practice changes a person.

Just as a tree grows every day, personality develops through regular actions.

The Seventh Principle

  • Development never ends.
  • Each stage of life opens up new challenges.
  • A person is capable of developing until the last day of his life.

Maturity is not an end point, but the ability to constantly learn, change, and remain open to new experiences.

DREVO practice formula

  • Every day ask yourself:
  • What did I do for my body today?
  • What new things have I learned?
  • What decision did I make consciously?
  • What strengthened my will?
  • What helped my soul open up?
  • What action strengthened my spirit?
  • Who did I help today?
  • What have I done for nature or the environment?
  • What did I create today?

If there is an honest answer to at least some of these questions, then this day has become a day of development.

The main principle of practice

Development is measured not by the amount of knowledge, but by the number of good, conscious and creative actions taken.

Real psychology does not begin in an office or in a book.

It begins at the moment when a person transforms understanding into action, action into habit, habit into character, and character into creative life.

Daily practices

Daily Development Practice in New Psychology DREVO

In the New Psychology DREVO, personal development is viewed not as a separate event, but as a way of life.

  • A person changes gradually.
  • Every day forms habits.
  • Habits shape character.
  • Character determines destiny.

Therefore, daily actions are much more important than occasional efforts.

The main goal of daily practice is to support the harmonious development of all levels of personality.

1. Body practice

Pay attention to your body every day.

This could be:

  • morning exercises;
  • walk;
  • stretching;
  • strength training;
  • breathing exercises;
  • good sleep;
  • healthy eating.

A strong body creates the basis for personal development.

2. Breathing practice

A few minutes of calm, conscious breathing can help reduce tension, restore focus, and improve your sense of well-being.

Breathing is one of the simplest ways to connect body and mind.

3. Mindfulness practice

  • Ask yourself a few questions every day.
  • What do I feel now?
  • What am I thinking about?
  • Why do I react this way?
  • What's really important today?

Mindfulness helps the consciousness manage life, rather than living automatically.

4. Practice gratitude

Every day, remember at least three things for which you can be grateful.

These could be:

  • People;
  • events;
  • health;→
  • nature;→
  • new knowledge;
  • own achievements.

Gratitude develops the ability to notice the value of life.

5. Teaching practice

  • Learn something new every day.
  • Read.
  • Learn languages.
  • Master the skills.
  • Ask questions.
  • The development of intelligence must continue throughout life.

6. Practice of creation

  • Create something useful every day.
  • Text.
  • Drawing.
  • Music.
  • Product.
  • An idea.
  • Solution to the problem.
  • Planted tree.
  • Helping a person.
  • Creation makes development real.

7. Practice good deeds

Every day, do at least one thing that will improve someone else's life.

This could be:

  • help;
  • support;
  • a kind word;
  • sincere conversation;
  • collaboration;
  • Gratitude.→

Personal development is impossible without the development of relationships.

8. Practice of nature

  • Find opportunities to interact with nature every day.
  • Walk.
  • Tree watching.
  • Working in the garden.
  • Contemplation of the sky.
  • Touching the ground.
  • Nature helps people restore their inner balance.

9. Practice of silence

Spend a few minutes every day without your phone, conversations, or information noise.

Silence helps you hear your own thoughts and feel your inner state.

It is in silence that the best solutions are often born.

10. Practice of meaning

  • Before going to bed, ask yourself three questions.
  • What good have I done today?
  • What did you learn today?
  • What will help me become a little better tomorrow?
  • These questions gradually shape the direction of personal development.

DREVO's Daily Development Formula

  • Every day it is necessary to strengthen:
  • Body— movement.
  • Psyche- calmness.
  • Consciousness- knowledge.
  • Will- discipline.
  • Soul- love.
  • Spirit- loyalty to one's values.
  • Relationship- good deeds.
  • Nature- care.
  • Society- cooperation.
  • Future- creation.

The main principle

  • It is impossible to change your life with one great act.
  • But it can be changed by thousands of small, correct actions.
  • Every day is a new seed.
  • Every habit is a new root.
  • Every good deed is a new branch.
  • Every creative decision is a new fruit.
  • This is how a strong Personality Tree gradually grows.

Definition

Daily practices are a system of regular actions aimed at the harmonious development of the body, psyche, consciousness, will, soul, spirit, social connections, and a person's relationship with nature and society.

In the New Psychology of DREVO, daily practice is considered the main tool for personal development, since it is repeated actions that gradually shape habits, character, and lifestyle.

A person doesn't become what they dream about. A person becomes what they do every day.

Weekly practices

Weekly Development Practice in New Psychology DREVO

While daily practices help a person maintain inner balance, weekly ones allow one to see the bigger picture of their life.

A week is a natural cycle of analysis, recovery and direction adjustment.

Each week, a person gets the opportunity to stop, evaluate their actions, strengthen relationships, recuperate, and take the next step forward.

1. Recovery Day

  • Make time for proper rest.
  • The body needs restoration no less than work.
  • Rest is not a waste of time.
  • It is a condition for further development.

2. Analysis of the week

  • Once a week, answer yourself these questions:
  • What went well?
  • What didn't work?
  • What have I learned?
  • What mistakes did you make?
  • What do I want to change next week?
  • Self-analysis helps transform experience into knowledge.

3. Setting goals

  • Identify a few key tasks for the next week.
  • It is important to choose goals that are not only work-related.
  • No less important are the goals:
  • health;→
  • families;
  • training;
  • creativity;
  • communication;
  • rest.

A harmonious life requires the development of all its aspects.

4. Time for family and friends

  • Spend time with the people who really matter.
  • Live communication builds trust.
  • Joint work.
  • Joint vacation.
  • Shared memories.
  • This is how strong relationships are born.

5. Practice of nature

  • Spend a few hours outside the urban environment.
  • Forest.
  • Park.
  • Lake.
  • Mountains.
  • Working in the garden.

Observing nature helps restore focus, reduce stress, and feel connected to the world around us.

6. Teaching practice

  • Take the time to learn a new topic in depth.
  • Not for the exam.
  • Not for the sake of the report.
  • For the sake of my own development.
  • Study what expands your understanding of the world.

7. Practice of creation

  • Create something new every week.
  • Write an article.
  • Plant a tree.
  • Develop a project.
  • Do something useful.
  • Help the community.
  • Creation turns knowledge into real change.

8. Practice of service

  • Do at least one selfless act.
  • Help the person.
  • Support the project.
  • Pass on the knowledge.
  • Become a mentor.
  • When a person makes the world a better place, not only society develops.
  • He himself is also developing.
