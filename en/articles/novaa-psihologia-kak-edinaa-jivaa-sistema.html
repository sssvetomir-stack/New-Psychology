Preamble

Humanity is entering a new era.

We're creating artificial intelligence, editing genomes, exploring space, and building a digital civilization. However, the central question remains:

Who is a man?

Modern psychology has helped us gain a deeper understanding of the brain, emotions, and behavior. But a person is more than just neurons, hormones, and cognitive processes. They are a living, multidimensional system in which the body, psyche, consciousness, will, soul, spirit, values, relationships, and the surrounding world continuously interact and influence one another.

This book offers a new perspective on human nature.

New Psychology DREVO Views personality not as a collection of individual functions or a set of psychological problems, but as a holistic system with enormous potential for development. Its goal is not only to help people cope with fear, anxiety, and internal conflicts, but also to show the path to maturity, freedom, creativity, and a meaningful life.

This book is based on a simple idea:

Man comes into the world not only to survive, but also to grow, love, collaborate, create and leave behind a legacy.

The ideas presented here integrate the achievements of modern science, philosophy, psychology, biology, pedagogy, and the centuries-old wisdom of humanity. Where scientific data exists, the book draws on it. Where the subject is soul, spirit, the meaning of life, and inner experience, it offers a philosophical model open to further exploration and development.

The symbol of this system is a tree.

A tree doesn't become strong overnight. It gradually strengthens its roots, forms a trunk, opens its crown, and bears fruit. A person develops in the same way. Their inner strength is born not from self-conquest, but from the harmonious development of all aspects of their personality.

This book is an invitation to explore your own nature.

Not to find ready-made answers to all questions, but to learn to ask the right questions, to better understand yourself, to understand other people more deeply, and to consciously participate in the development of life.

Human development is the development of society. The development of society is the development of civilization. The development of civilization begins with the development of each of us.

New psychology asa single living system.

In the traditional worldview of the Slavs, man was viewed not as a set of separate organs or psychological processes, but asa single living system, in which body, soul, spirit, race and nature are interconnected.

Several principles can be identified.

Soul— the source of feelings, conscience, love, creativity, and the inner drive to live. It was perceived as what makes a person truly alive.

Spirit— will, purpose, striving upward, the ability to overcome fear and follow the chosen path. If the soul senses, the spirit guides.

Body— the instrument through which the soul and spirit act in the world. Caring for the body was not an end in itself, but a condition for a full life.

Humanity's relationship with the surrounding world was also of great importance. Forests, rivers, land, and the sun were perceived not simply as resources, but as part of the overall order of life. Hence the respect for nature, seasonal cycles, and labor.

Interestingly, many modern branches of psychology are returning to similar ideas, albeit using different language. For example:

  • psychosomatics studies the connection between mental states and the body;
  • Ecopsychology studies the influence of nature on mental state;
  • Positive psychology examines meaning, values, and the inner development of a person.

It's important to distinguish between historical facts and modern interpretations. Few direct written sources remain on pre-Christian Slavic beliefs, so many details are reconstructed from folklore, archaeology, linguistics, and comparisons with other Indo-European traditions. Consequently, some modern concepts of "Slavic psychology" are more philosophical reconstructions than proven descriptions of ancient views.

If we look at it more broadly, we can formulate the following idea:

The psychology of the future may become not only a science of human behavior, but also a science of the harmony of the body, consciousness, personality, relationships, and what various philosophical and religious traditions call the soul.

Psychology of the future is the science of human harmony.

Psychology of the Futuremay become much broader than modern psychology. If today it mainly studies behavior, emotions, thinking, and brain function, then in the future it may turn intothe science of holistic human development.

This concept can be represented as follows.

Psychology of the future is the science of human harmony.

A person is considered as a single system consisting of several interconnected levels:

  • body - health, movement, nutrition, physiology;
  • psyche - emotions, memory, thinking, character;
  • consciousness - attention, awareness, ability to learn;
  • values ​​and meaning - worldview, motivation, life goals;
  • social connections - family, community, culture;
  • environment - nature, city, technology.

In this model, the psychologist's role changes. They become not only a specialist in treating mental health issues but also help people develop their abilities, build lasting relationships, develop their thinking, and find meaning.

New directions

The psychology of the future may combine the achievements of many sciences:

  • neurobiology;
  • cognitive science;
  • medicine;
  • pedagogy;
  • sociology;
  • artificial intelligence;
  • human ecology.

However, any claims about consciousness, the effectiveness of methods, or the impact of practices must be tested by research and not accepted without evidence.

The main goal

The main goal is not just getting rid of anxiety or depression, but the development of a person in all his or her full potential:

  • physical health;→
  • emotional stability;→
  • critical thinking;→
  • ability to cooperate;
  • creativity;
  • a sense of responsibility to society and nature.

Possible wording

Future psychology is an interdisciplinary science of human development that integrates knowledge about the brain, mind, body, consciousness, social relationships, and interactions with the environment to improve quality of life and unlock human potential.

This approach does not reject modern scientific methods, but rather expands them by integrating data from different fields of knowledge and maintaining the requirement to test ideas through empirical research.

Foundations of the New Psychology

The new psychology can view humans not as the bearer of symptoms or a set of cognitive processes, but as a holistic, evolving system. It integrates knowledge from the natural, social, and human sciences, maintaining a critical approach to hypotheses and relying on verifiable data where possible.

1. Human integrity A person is a unity of body, psyche, consciousness, relationships with others, and the environment. Changes in one area can impact others.

2. Development instead of eliminating problems Psychology helps not only overcome difficulties but also develop strengths: resilience, creativity, the ability to learn, collaborate, and make decisions.

3. Meaning and values Understanding your own values ​​and life goals impacts well-being, motivation, and ability to cope with challenges.

4. Unity of reason and emotions Emotions and thinking work together. Emotions convey information, and rational thinking helps us understand and use it.

5. The body and mind are interconnected Sleep, nutrition, physical activity, illness, and stress all affect psychological well-being, just as mental well-being can affect physical well-being.

6. Man is a part of society and nature Mental well-being is associated with the quality of relationships, a sense of belonging, a safe environment, and contact with nature.

7. Awareness and responsibility A person is able to observe their thoughts, emotions and habits, gradually changing their behavior through conscious choice.

8. Scientific verifiability Any new theory must be testable. Ideas concerning consciousness, spiritual experience, or new methods of assistance can be explored, but they should not be declared established facts without sufficient evidence.

9. Individuality There is no one-size-fits-all developmental path. Assistance methods must take into account individual characteristics, culture, age, and life experience.

10. Continuous development Psychological development continues throughout life. The goal is not to achieve an "ideal state," but to be able to learn, adapt, and maintain internal resilience in the face of change.

Such a system can be considered as the basis of an interdisciplinary psychology that combines the achievements of modern science with philosophical questions about personality, the meaning of life, and human development, without mixing ideological ideas with established scientific knowledge.
