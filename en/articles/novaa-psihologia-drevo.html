Man as a living ecosystem

Introduction

The new psychology of DREVO is a philosophical and scientific concept that views humans as integral living systems, constantly interacting with themselves, society, nature, and the environment.

Name WOODIt was not chosen by chance.

A tree is one of the most perfect forms of life. It has strong roots, a powerful trunk, a developed crown, continuously exchanges matter and energy with the surrounding world, grows, regenerates, and bears fruit.

  • A person develops according to the same laws.
  • It is impossible to strengthen the crown if the roots are destroyed.
  • It is impossible to have a strong trunk without healthy soil.
  • It is impossible to obtain fruits without a long growing process.

This is why the new psychology views a person as a living system, and not as a collection of individual functions.

The basic principle

Everything in a person is interconnected.

Changes in one part of the system inevitably affect the others.

Personality development occurs not through the struggle of individual parts of a person, but through their harmonious interaction.

Ten levels of man

1. Body

The foundation of life.

A source of biological energy, health and interaction with the environment.

2. Subconscious

  • Deep memory of the body.
  • Automatic life programs.
  • Instincts.
  • Bodily memory.
  • Hereditary traits.
  • Biological adaptation.

3. Psyche

  • The system of perception and processing of information.
  • Emotions.
  • Memory.
  • Thinking.
  • Imagination.
  • Training.

4. Consciousness

Space of observation and free choice.

Consciousness connects all levels of a person and allows him to consciously direct his life.

5. Superconsciousness

A source of creativity, inspiration, deep insights and the search for new opportunities.

The superconscious is considered as an area whose nature requires further philosophical and scientific research.

6. Soul

The inner core of personality.

The source of love, conscience, beauty, compassion and a sense of self-authenticity.

7. Spirit

  • Vector of human development.
  • Will.
  • Purpose.
  • The ability to follow your values ​​even in difficult circumstances.

8. Values ​​and meaning

Inner compass of personality.

They determine not only a person’s goals, but also the ways to achieve them.

9. Social connections

  • A person develops through relationships.
  • Family.
  • Community.
  • Culture.
  • Education.
  • Joint creativity.

10. Interaction with nature and the environment

Man is part of a single ecosystem of life.

Nature, society and technology shape the conditions of its development, and man, in turn, influences them with his decisions and actions.

The principle of psychological ecology

  • The inner world of a person is like a living ecosystem.
  • Thoughts.
  • Emotions.
  • Information.
  • Relationship.
  • Food.
  • Music.
  • Culture.
  • Nature.
  • All of them are an environment in which personality develops.

Just as environmental pollution destroys forests, informational, emotional and social pollution can disrupt a person’s inner balance.

Therefore, caring for psychological health begins with caring for the quality of the environment in which a person lives.

The principle of growth

A tree never grows by destroying its own roots.

Likewise, a person cannot develop by destroying his body, psyche, relationships or environment.

True development always strengthens the system as a whole.

The principle of interrelation

  • The body influences the psyche.
  • The psyche influences consciousness.
  • Consciousness influences actions.
  • Actions shape character.
  • Character influences society.
  • Society changes the environment.
  • The environment influences people again.
  • Development occurs in a circle, forming a continuous cycle of life.

The main goal

The new psychology of DREVO is aimed not only at overcoming psychological difficulties.

Its goal is to help people become more holistic, conscious, healthy, and responsible, to discover their potential, and to learn to live in harmony with themselves, other people, and the world around them.

Motto

Strong roots. Clear consciousness. Living soul. Creative spirit. Harmony with the world.

The new psychology of DREVO views human development as a tree's growth: the deeper the roots, the higher the crown can rise. True development begins not with self-defeat, but with understanding one's nature, respect for life, and the desire to create conditions in which not only the individual but also society as a whole can grow.

Definition of a healthy person

A healthy person— is a person whose body, psyche, consciousness, values, relationships with people and interaction with the environment are in a state of dynamic harmony, allowing him to live fully, develop, love, create and take responsibility for his actions.

Health is more than just the absence of illness. It is a person's ability to maintain inner balance, recover from difficulties, make informed decisions, and realize one's potential.

In the New Psychology of DREVO, human health is manifested in several interconnected dimensions.

Physical health

The body has sufficient vital energy, is capable of movement, recovery and adaptation to changing conditions.

Mental health

The psyche flexibly perceives reality, regulates emotions, learns from experience, and helps a person effectively interact with the surrounding world.

Mindfulness

Consciousness allows a person to understand themselves, distinguish between automatic reactions and conscious decisions, take responsibility for their own lives and maintain the ability to make free choices.

Value integrity

A person's actions are consistent with their deepest values. There is consistency between what they think, say, and do.

Social health

A person is capable of building relationships based on respect, trust, cooperation, and mutual responsibility. They know how to receive support and become its provider.

Environmental health

People recognize their interconnectedness with nature, society, and technology. They strive not to destroy the environment of which they are a part, but to support its sustainability and development.

Ability to develop

A healthy person is not "complete." They retain the ability to learn, reflect on experiences, acknowledge mistakes, change, and develop new abilities throughout their lives.

The main criterion of health

The main sign of health isnot the perfection of a person, but his ability to maintain and restore harmony within himself and in relationships with the outside world.

A healthy person is able to:

  • to love without losing one's own dignity;
  • to be free without denying responsibility;
  • maintain internal stability in conditions of uncertainty;
  • collaborate without losing individuality;
  • create more life than destruction;
  • leave behind people, communities, and the environment in better condition than they found them before his involvement.

In DREVO's New Psychology, health is not a final state, but a continuous process of harmonious development of individuals, society, and nature.

Practical methods of personal development

The TREVO principle

Human development begins not with a struggle against oneself, but with strengthening all levels of one’s life.

Just as a tree cannot grow only through its branches or only through its roots, so a person develops harmoniously only when the body, psyche, consciousness, values, relationships and interaction with the surrounding world develop.

The new psychology of DREVO offers a practice of continuous development through ten interconnected areas.

1. Body development

The basis of personal development is a healthy body.

Practices:

  • good sleep;
  • natural nutrition;
  • movement and exercise;
  • breathing practices;
  • massage and body therapy;
  • hardening;
  • nature walks;
  • recovery after exercise.

The main goal is to increase the body's vital resources.

2. Development of the subconscious

The subconscious changes through repetition of experience.

Practices:

  • formation of healthy habits;
  • working with automatic reactions;
  • development of physical sensitivity;
  • conscious breathing;
  • keeping a diary of observations;
  • working with emotional triggers;
  • learning new skills to the point of automatism.

The main goal is to make internal programs allies of a person.

3. Development of the psyche

The psyche develops through diverse life experiences.

Practices:

  • training;
  • reading;
  • solving complex problems;
  • development of emotional literacy;
  • creative activity;→
  • development of memory and attention;
  • learning foreign languages;
  • music and art.

The main goal is to increase flexibility of thinking and emotional stability.

4. Development of consciousness

Consciousness develops through observation and conscious choice.

Practices:

  • reflection;
  • meditation or other mindfulness practices;
  • keeping a diary;
  • analysis of own decisions;
  • goal setting;
  • development of critical thinking;
  • regular self-education.

The main goal is to increase freedom of choice.

5. Development of the superconscious

The superconscious is revealed through creativity, the search for something new and deep attention to one’s own experience.

Practices:

  • creative projects;
  • research activities;
  • free writing;
  • contemplation of nature;
  • working with symbols and images;
  • development of intuitive thinking with subsequent testing of emerging ideas;
  • participation in scientific and philosophical discussions.

The main goal is to expand the ability to see new possibilities.

6. Soul development

The soul develops through the experience of human values.

Practices:

  • Gratitude;→
  • helping others;
  • art;
  • sincere communication;
  • development of compassion;
  • maintaining internal integrity;
  • search for personal meaning.

The main goal is to maintain internal integrity.

7. Development of the spirit

The spirit is strengthened through action.

Practices:

  • fulfillment of given promises;
  • discipline;→
  • voluntary overcoming of difficulties;
  • service to society;
  • long-term projects;
  • development of courage and responsibility.

The main goal is to connect beliefs and actions.

8. Development of values

Values ​​become reality only when they are manifested in everyday life.

Practices:

  • regularly reflecting on your decisions;
  • discussion of moral issues;
  • participation in socially useful projects;
  • development of a culture of communication;
  • learning from life experience.

The main goal is to align worldview and lifestyle.

9. Development of social connections

Personality develops through cooperation.

Practices:

  • creating a family;
  • friendship;
  • participation in communities;
  • joint creativity;
  • teaching;
  • teaching others;
  • conflict resolution through dialogue.

The main goal is to create relationships in which each participant develops.

10. Development of interaction with the environment

A person develops along with the world that he creates around himself.

Practices:

  • contact with nature;
  • eco-friendly lifestyle;
  • participation in environmental restoration;
  • intelligent use of technology;
  • creating a beautiful and harmonious space;
  • development of environmental culture.

The main goal is to leave the world better than you found it.
