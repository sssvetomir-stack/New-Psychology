The Main Question of a Mature Personality in New Psychology DREVO

At a certain point in life, a person begins to ask himself a question that goes far beyond personal success:

  • What will remain after me?
  • This question becomes one of the signs of maturity.
  • The child thinks about what to get.
  • An adult talks about what to save.
  • A mature person begins to think about what to leave behind.

It is with this question that the transition from living for oneself to living for the future begins.

Why is this question so important?

Every person's life is limited.

But the consequences of his actions can last for decades and even centuries.

  • Every person becomes a link between the past and the future.
  • He receives the legacy of previous generations.
  • And creates a legacy for those who follow.
  • Therefore, the main question is not only how long a person lived.
  • And that it will continue to live thanks to him.

What's really left?

  • Not everything a person accumulates becomes an inheritance.
  • True legacy is created where life continues through our actions.
  • Remaining:
  • The love we gave.
  • Knowledge shared.
  • People who were inspired.
  • Children who were raised.
  • Students who were helped to open up.
  • Communities that united.
  • Trees that were planted.
  • Projects that continue to work.
  • Culture that has been preserved or created.
  • Goodness that has become a habit for other people.

Seven questions to ask yourself

  • It is useful to regularly ask:
  • What kind of person will I be remembered as?
  • What has become better thanks to me?
  • What knowledge have I passed on?
  • Who did I make stronger?
  • What have I created with my own hands or my own mind?
  • What kind of world will remain after my life?
  • Will people's lives be easier because I was here?

The answers to these questions gradually determine the direction of your entire life.

The legacy begins today

  • Legacy is not created on the last day of life.
  • It is created daily.
  • Every conversation.
  • Every decision.
  • Every book.
  • Every lesson.
  • Every tree.
  • Every idea.
  • Every honest act becomes part of the future.
  • Therefore, the question of legacy is a question of today.

The highest form of success

  • In the New Psychology of DREVO, success has several levels.
  • The first is to learn to provide for yourself.
  • The second is to learn to be happy.
  • The third is to help others become happy.

The highest form of success is to create a system that continues to benefit people even without the constant involvement of its creator.

This is when personal life becomes a legacy.

Heritage Practice

Once a month, it is helpful to write down the answers to three questions.

  • What new have I created?
  • Who did you help grow?
  • What have I done that will be useful in ten years?
  • This practice gradually changes your outlook on life.
  • A person begins to make decisions not only for today.
  • But also for the sake of future generations.

The main principle

The greatest legacy is not what a man leaves to his children.

His greatest legacy is the world that is made better by his life.

Definition

The question "What will be left after me?" is a question about personal legacy, the meaning of life, and responsibility to future generations. It helps a person transcend personal interests and begin to build a life whose results continue to be beneficial long after their passing.

In the New Psychology of DREVO, this question is considered one of the signs of personal maturity. It guides a person toward creation, service, knowledge sharing, social development, and environmental conservation.

When a person stops asking only, "What will I get?" and begins asking, "What will remain after me?", their life takes on a new dimension. At that moment, they cease to be simply a participant in history and begin to become its creator.
