The new psychology can view humans not as the bearer of symptoms or a set of cognitive processes, but as a holistic, evolving system. It integrates knowledge from the natural, social, and human sciences, maintaining a critical approach to hypotheses and relying on verifiable data where possible.

1. Human integrity A person is a unity of body, psyche, consciousness, relationships with others, and the environment. Changes in one area can impact others.

2. Development instead of eliminating problems Psychology helps not only overcome difficulties but also develop strengths: resilience, creativity, the ability to learn, collaborate, and make decisions.

3. Meaning and values Understanding your own values ​​and life goals impacts well-being, motivation, and ability to cope with challenges.

4. Unity of reason and emotions Emotions and thinking work together. Emotions convey information, and rational thinking helps us understand and use it.

5. The body and mind are interconnected Sleep, nutrition, physical activity, illness, and stress all affect psychological well-being, just as mental well-being can affect physical well-being.

6. Man is a part of society and nature Mental well-being is associated with the quality of relationships, a sense of belonging, a safe environment, and contact with nature.

7. Awareness and responsibility A person is able to observe their thoughts, emotions and habits, gradually changing their behavior through conscious choice.

8. Scientific verifiability Any new theory must be testable. Ideas concerning consciousness, spiritual experience, or new methods of assistance can be explored, but they should not be declared established facts without sufficient evidence.

9. Individuality There is no one-size-fits-all developmental path. Assistance methods must take into account individual characteristics, culture, age, and life experience.

10. Continuous development Psychological development continues throughout life. The goal is not to achieve an "ideal state," but to be able to learn, adapt, and maintain internal resilience in the face of change.

Such a system can be considered as the basis of an interdisciplinary psychology that combines the achievements of modern science with philosophical questions about personality, the meaning of life, and human development, without mixing ideological ideas with established scientific knowledge.

MANIFESTO OF THE NEW PSYCHOLOGY

Science of the Whole Man

Humanity is entering an era when old ideas about man are no longer sufficient.

Modern psychology has made great strides in studying behavior, emotions, memory, thinking, and brain function. However, humans are far more complex than a set of neural processes or behavioral patterns.

New psychology proposes to view a person as a holistic living system in which the body, psyche, consciousness, values, relationships, environment, and internal search for meaning are interconnected.

We recognize the achievements of modern science and consider them the foundation for further development. At the same time, we are open to exploring questions that have not yet received definitive answers: the nature of consciousness, subjective experience, creativity, intuition, the meaning of life, and other profound aspects of human existence.

Basic principles

We argue that a person is an integral system that includes several interconnected levels.

Body— the foundation of life, possessing its own biological wisdom and the ability to self-regulate.

Subconscious— a deep system of automatic processes, experiential memory, hereditary programs and bodily adaptation.

Psyche— a system of perception, thinking, emotions, memory, learning and interaction with the surrounding world.

Consciousness- the ability to observe, perceive, discern and freely choose the direction of action.

Superconsciousness— a space of creativity, inspiration, deep insights, and the search for new possibilities. Its nature requires further scientific research.

Soul— a philosophical concept denoting the inner core of a person, their values, sense of authenticity, and experience of meaning.

Spirit— a philosophical concept denoting a person’s striving for ideals, the ability to remain faithful to one’s principles and overcome difficulties for the sake of the chosen path.

Values

We believe that human development is impossible without an internal moral foundation.

  • Love.
  • Freedom.
  • Responsibility.
  • Dignity.
  • Honesty.
  • Justice.
  • Creativity.
  • Cooperation.
  • Respect for life.
  • Creation.

These values ​​do not pit people against each other, but create the basis for the joint development of the individual, society and civilization.

Community

  • A person develops through relationships.
  • Family.
  • Friendship.
  • Genus.
  • Culture.
  • Education.
  • Scientific cooperation.
  • Society.

Psychological health cannot be considered separately from the quality of human relationships.

Nature

Man is a part of nature.

Its well-being cannot be separated from the state of water, air, soil, forests, animals and the entire ecosystem of the Earth.

Caring for nature is also caring for people.

Technologies

Technologies should expand human capabilities, not replace humans.

Artificial intelligence should contribute to the development of personality, education, science, and medicine, while preserving human freedom of choice, dignity, and responsibility.

Scientific approach

New psychology does not reject science.

It expands the field of research.

Anything that can be verified should be investigated by scientific methods.

Anything that does not yet have sufficient evidence must remain the subject of open, honest and critical study, and not of unconditional belief or denial.

The main goal

  • The main goal of the new psychology is not only to reduce suffering.
  • The main goal is to unlock human potential.
  • Help a person become healthier.
  • More free.
  • More conscious.
  • More responsible.
  • More creative.
  • More capable of love.

More capable of creating a world in which future generations can live with dignity.

Our vision

We see a person not as a problem that needs to be fixed.

We see man as a continuously evolving system, capable of knowing, creating, loving, cooperating and transforming the world.

The new psychology is not a rejection of the past.

This is the next stage of understanding man.

A stage in which science, philosophy, culture and human experience do not oppose each other, but together help to more deeply understand the nature of the individual and its place in the world.

By knowing a person, we know the world.

By changing ourselves, we change the future.

The principle of psychological ecology

The inner world of a person is like a living ecosystem

New psychology views a person as a complex living ecosystem in which the body, psyche, consciousness, subconscious, superconscious, soul, spirit, values, social connections and the environment form a single interconnected system.

Just as environmental pollution disrupts the functioning of ecosystems, informational, emotional and social pollution disrupts a person’s internal balance.

Psychological ecology is the science and practice of maintaining a healthy internal human environment.

Basic Law

Everything that a person lets into himself gradually becomes part of his inner world.

This applies not only to food and air, but also to:

  • information;→
  • words;
  • emotions;→
  • music;
  • images;
  • human communication;
  • digital environment;
  • habits;
  • culture;→
  • knowledge;→
  • daily thoughts.

Every external signal becomes a kind of food for the psyche.

Therefore, a person must take care not only of the cleanliness of his body, but also of the cleanliness of his internal environment.

Psychological pollution

Sources of psychological pollution can be:

  • constant fear;
  • hatred;
  • aggression;
  • lie;
  • manipulation;
  • information noise;
  • chronic stress;
  • destructive addictions;
  • humiliation of dignity;
  • toxic relationship.

Long-term exposure to such factors can reduce psychological stability, impair quality of life, and hinder personal development.

Psychological nutrition

Just as the body needs healthy food, the psyche needs quality psychological nutrition.

These include:

  • Love;→
  • respect;→
  • knowledge;→
  • creativity;
  • beauty;→
  • music;
  • nature;→
  • friendship;
  • honest dialogue;
  • sense of meaning;
  • training;
  • physical activity;
  • rest;
  • silence.

Psychological nutrition creates conditions for internal growth.

Internal immunity

The task of the new psychology is not to isolate a person from the world, but to develop his internal stability.

Psychological immunity includes the ability to:

  • critically evaluate information;
  • recognize manipulation;
  • maintain emotional balance;
  • recover from difficulties;
  • preserve your own values;
  • build healthy relationships.

The stronger the internal immunity, the less destructive the impact of an unfavorable environment.

Ecological principle of development

A person develops most harmoniously when his inner world is in harmony with the surrounding world.

Caring for nature, the health of society and one’s own internal state are different manifestations of the same process – the preservation and development of life.

Therefore, the new psychology considers ecology not only as an attitude towards nature, but also as a principle of thinking, communication, education, upbringing and culture.

The main principle

  • Everything is interconnected.
  • Thoughts influence emotions.
  • Emotions affect the body.
  • The body influences the mind.
  • Consciousness influences actions.
  • Actions change society.
  • Society changes the environment.
  • The environment influences people again.

Understanding this interconnection, a person ceases to perceive himself as a separate being and begins to recognize himself as part of a single system of life.

Psychological ecology is the art of maintaining the purity of one's inner world while simultaneously creating a healthier world around oneself.
