3.7 Shame

ShameShame is a feeling associated with the awareness of one's own inadequacy to expectations, both one's own and others'. It can be related to mistakes, actions, or even personality traits. The TREE concept views shame as a tool for self-knowledge and growth, helping one recognize one's boundaries and values, and learn to be sincere with oneself and others.

The nature of shame

  • The emotion of social connection
  • Shame arises in the context of relationships with other people, when a person feels that his actions or qualities may be condemned.
  • Imperfection signal
  • Shame helps us to recognize our mistakes or those aspects that require attention and development.
  • Threat to self-acceptance
  • Unresolved shame can develop into a feeling of unworthiness, preventing a person from living freely and harmoniously.

Why is it important to work with shame?

  • Reducing internal stress
  • Consciously working with shame helps you avoid self-condemnation and strengthen your self-esteem.
  • Self-acceptance
  • Overcoming shame helps develop self-love, even in the face of imperfection.
  • Strengthening connections with others
  • Openness and sincerity help build deep and trusting relationships.

How to recognize shame?

  • Confession of shame
  • Tell yourself:"I feel shame, and that's normal. This feeling points to something important to me."
  • Studying the cause
  • Ask yourself:What is causing my shame? Is it related to my values ​​or to external expectations?
  • Separation of self and actions
  • Realize that being ashamed of your actions doesn't make you a bad person. Your actions don't equal your personality.

Practice of working with shame

  • Self-Compassion
  • Remind yourself that everyone makes mistakes.
  • Use the phrase:"I admit my mistake and learn from it. I am worthy of respect and love."
  • Correcting the situation
  • If possible, correct your actions: apologize, explain, or offer help to restore trust.
  • Reflection and lessons
  • Write down what you learned from the embarrassing situation and what steps you can take to avoid similar situations in the future.
  • Reducing the influence of external expectations
  • Ask yourself:Is this shame caused by my true values ​​or imposed standards?Work on letting go of judging yourself for not living up to other people's expectations.

An example of working with shame

  • SituationYou feel ashamed for making a mistake on an important project.
  • Awareness: You understand that shame is caused by your desire for perfection.
  • ActionYou apologize to your colleagues, explain what happened, and offer a plan to correct the situation.

Meditation for dealing with shame

  • Sit comfortably, close your eyes and take a few deep breaths.
  • Imagine your shame as a dark cloud inside you.
  • With each exhalation, visualize the cloud becoming lighter and more transparent.
  • Tell yourself:"I accept myself as I am. I learn and grow through my mistakes."

What to Avoid When Working with Shame

  • Suppression of shame
  • Suppressed shame can develop into a chronic feeling of unworthiness.
  • Insulation
  • Don't isolate yourself from others out of shame. Instead, find someone you can confide in.
  • Judging yourself too harshly
  • Remind yourself that mistakes are part of the human experience and they do not define your worth.

Shame as a resource in TREE

  • Awareness of valuesShame helps you understand what aspects of life are important to you.
  • Lessons of sincerityWorking with shame teaches you to be honest with yourself and others.
  • The power of vulnerabilityBeing open about your experiences builds trust and helps build deep connections.

Conclusion

Shame is not an obstacle, but an opportunity. In the TREE concept, it becomes a tool that helps one recognize one's values, learn to accept oneself, and transform mistakes into lessons. By consciously working with shame, one develops inner resilience, sincerity, and harmony with oneself and the world around oneself.
