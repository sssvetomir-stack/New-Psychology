Awareness of emotion

Awareness of emotion is the first and most important step in working with feelings. This process involves noticing the emotion as it arises, understanding its nature, and its impact on your state. Only by becoming aware of what you're feeling can you begin to work constructively with it.

Why is it important to be aware of emotions?

  • Interruption of automatic reactions
  • Recognizing an emotion gives you the opportunity to stop and think before acting impulsively.
  • Understanding your needs
  • Emotions often indicate unmet needs or internal conflicts.
  • State management
  • By noticing your feelings, you can take steps to stabilize your emotional state.
  • Improving relationships
  • When you are aware of your emotions, you can more easily explain them to others, which strengthens mutual understanding.

How to be aware of emotions?

1. Notice the emotion

  • Pay attention to your internal state. Ask yourself questions:
  • "What am I feeling now?"
  • "Why do I feel this?"

2. Feel your body

  • Emotions are often expressed through physical sensations:
  • Anger: tension in the shoulders, increased heart rate.
  • Fear: tightness in the stomach, coldness in the extremities.
  • Joy: lightness, warmth in the chest.
  • Notice where in your body you feel the emotion.

3. Name the emotion

  • Give your emotion a name. This helps you separate it from yourself and look at it objectively.
  • "I feel sad."
  • "It's an irritation."
  • "I'm happy now."

4. Accept the emotion without judgment

  • Recognize that emotion is a natural reaction to a situation. It's neither good nor bad—it's simply how you feel right now.
  • "It's normal that I'm angry right now."
  • "I allow myself to feel it."

Practice of Emotional Awareness

1. The Pause Method

  • When you notice yourself starting to experience a strong emotion, pause:
  • Close your eyes.
  • Take a few deep breaths.
  • Focus on your body and emotions.

2. Emotional diary

  • Write down your feelings every day:
  • What did you feel?
  • At what point did this happen?
  • What was the reason?
  • This will help you better understand your emotional reactions.

3. Mindfulness Meditation

  • Spend 5-10 minutes a day practicing observing your thoughts and emotions.
  • Notice what comes into your consciousness without trying to change anything.

An example of awareness of emotion

Situation: You received critical feedback at work.

  • Notice the emotion:
  • "I feel irritated and resentful."
  • Feel the body:
  • "I notice tension in my chest and stiffness in my shoulders."
  • Name the emotion:
  • "It's irritation because I wanted to hear praise, not criticism."
  • Accept the emotion:
  • "It's normal that I feel this way. It shows that I value my colleagues' opinions."

Tips for Effective Emotional Awareness

  • Check your condition regularly
  • Take pauses throughout the day to ask yourself:"How am I feeling right now?"
  • Avoid self-condemnation
  • Remember that emotions are a natural part of you, not something that needs to be fixed.
  • Be patient
  • Understanding emotions takes time and practice. Don't rush the process.

Conclusion

Recognizing your emotions is the first step to transforming them. Understanding what you feel and why gives you the freedom to choose how to respond. This skill strengthens your resilience, makes your life more mindful, and helps you build harmonious relationships with yourself and the world around you.
