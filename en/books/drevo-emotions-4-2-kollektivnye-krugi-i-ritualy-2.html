4.2 Collective circles and rituals

Collective circles and ritualsIn the TREE concept, they are a powerful tool for working with emotions and energies in a group. They create a safe space for interaction, strengthen mutual support, and promote healing and growth through communication, shared action, and collective intentions.

Why are collective circles and rituals necessary?

  • Creating a space of trust
  • Participants feel heard and understood, which promotes deep opening.
  • Strengthening ties
  • Working together creates a sense of unity and mutual support.
  • Transformation through collective energy
  • The energy of the group enhances the effect of working with emotions and helps to achieve inner balance.
  • Supporting awareness
  • In the circle, participants gain new insights into their emotions through communication and feedback.

Principles of organizing circles and rituals

  • Non-judgmental
  • In the circle, no one judges or criticizes other participants.
  • Draw
  • Every participant has the right to be heard, regardless of their experience or status.
  • Confidentiality
  • Everything that happens in the circle stays in the circle.
  • Mindfulness
  • Participants share their feelings and thoughts sincerely, without hiding or embellishing them.

Formats of collective circles

  • Circles of trust
  • Target: Create space for open expression of emotions and experiences.
  • How to conduct:
  • Participants sit in a circle. One participant speaks, the others listen without interrupting.
  • The leader can suggest a topic for the circle, for example:How I Deal with Anger or What brings me joy.
  • Result: A deep sense of support and understanding.
  • Circles of gratitude
  • Target: Focus on the positive aspects of life and express gratitude to others.
  • How to conduct:
  • Each participant shares something they are grateful for in their life or thanks another participant for their support.
  • Result: Improves mood and strengthens bonds within the group.
  • Circles of intentions
  • Target: Formulating a collective intention or goal.
  • How to conduct:
  • Participants share their individual goals and combine them into a collective intention.
  • For example: "We create space for harmony and support for each other."
  • Result: Strengthening the energy of the group to achieve goals.

Rituals of transformation

  • Relaxation ritual
  • Target: Release old emotions, grievances or thoughts.
  • How to conduct:
  • Participants write down on paper what they wish to release. These papers are then burned in a fire or ritually released into water.
  • Result: Releasing emotional burden and creating space for something new.
  • Ritual of forgiveness
  • Target: Work with resentments and hatred to come to forgiveness.
  • How to conduct:
  • Participants close their eyes and visualize the person or situation that caused the pain.
  • Each participant says:"I forgive you and free myself from this pain."
  • Result: Inner healing and a feeling of lightness.
  • Ritual of gratitude to the elements
  • Target: Interacting with the energy of nature to restore harmony.
  • How to conduct:
  • Participants gather in nature, interacting with water, earth, fire and air through touch, breath or symbolic actions.
  • Result: Feeling connected to nature and restoring inner balance.
  • Ritual of Unity
  • Target: Strengthen a collective sense of unity and support.
  • How to conduct:
  • Participants join hands or form a circle, passing symbolic energy to each other through words or actions.
  • Result: Strengthening collective energy and mutual trust.

The role of the leading circle

  • Creating a safe space
  • The leader sets the rules of the circle: respect, confidentiality, equality.
  • Process management
  • He keeps track of time and ensures that all participants participate equally.
  • Support for participants
  • The facilitator helps participants express their emotions and directs the energy of the group.

Circle of Trust Example: Working with Anger

  • Theme of the circle: "How I work with anger."
  • Conducting:
  • Each participant takes turns sharing their experiences of working with anger.
  • The presenter suggests an exercise: visualize anger as a flow of energy and direct it toward creation.
  • Result: Realizing that anger can be a source of strength and inspiration.

Conclusion

Collective circles and rituals are an essential element of the TREE concept. They create a space for genuine interaction, mutual support, and deep healing. Such practices enhance collective energy, help transform emotions, and strengthen a sense of unity. In the group, each participant not only works on themselves but also becomes part of the overall process of growth and harmony.
